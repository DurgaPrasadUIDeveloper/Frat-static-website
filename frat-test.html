FRAT Test Guide

What is the FRAT IgG Test?

The FRAT test (also known as the folate receptor autoantibody test) helps assess conditions linked to impaired folate metabolism. It may be used in pregnancy care and developmental evaluations. It is not a standalone test for autism.

The FRAT blood test helps to

  • Detect folate receptor autoantibodies in blood
  • Assess the risk of impaired folate transport into cells

Support evaluation of

  • Recurrent pregnancy loss (infertility blood tests for women)
  • Neural tube defects
  • Autism-related evaluation, as one part of a wider assessment
  • Unexplained developmental delays in children
  • Neurodevelopmental concerns

What is the FRAT test for?

The FRAT test (folate receptor autoantibody test) is a blood-based test that screens both blocking and binding autoantibodies, which may affect folate transport to the brain and CSF.

Why does the FRAT test matter?

The FRAT test can flag folate-related issues that may be linked to fertility concerns and developmental delays, so a doctor can consider next steps early.

Who should get the FRAT test done?

The FRAT test may be considered for women facing fertility issues or pregnancy loss, and for children with developmental delays. Your doctor decides whether it is right for you.

What does a positive FRAT test mean?

A positive result means folate transport may be impaired. Your doctor will decide on next steps, which may include treatment, supplements, and follow-up tests.

How is the FRAT test done?

A simple blood sample is taken to detect folate receptor autoantibodies affecting cellular folate uptake. The FRAT blood test involves a simple blood draw, analysed for folate receptor autoantibodies.

Time: the blood sample collection is usually completed within 15-20 minutes.

Preparation: no fasting is needed for a FRAT blood test. Follow basic instructions from your FRAT test lab for accurate results.
